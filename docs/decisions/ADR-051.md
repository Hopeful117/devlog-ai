# ADR-051 — Trusted Knowledge Duplicate Policy

## Status

Accepted

## Date

2026-08-12

## Context

DevLog AI now supports incremental knowledge evolution for architecture analysis
(ADR-050).

That evolution reduces the chance that repeated analysis will restate the same
knowledge, but it does not yet define a complete policy for duplicate and
near-duplicate knowledge across the broader lifecycle.

Today, DevLog already handles several forms of technical duplication:

* repository-evidence deduplication;
* deterministic bounded selection;
* callback idempotence;
* prompt / context digests;
* duplicate engineering-event rejection within one result set.

Those controls are useful, but they are not the same problem as **trusted
knowledge duplication**.

The unresolved question is not whether repeated semantic content can appear
anywhere in the system.

The real question is:

> Where is repeated semantic content acceptable, and where does it become a
> defect?

This distinction matters because DevLog has at least two different persistence
surfaces with different responsibilities:

1. immutable proposal history (`ValidatableProposal`) governed by ADR-006;
2. trusted project knowledge (`Insight` and related trusted entities) consumed
   by humans and future machine workflows.

Without an explicit policy, duplicate handling degrades into:

* human vigilance during validation;
* ad hoc heuristics;
* inconsistent repository behavior across Story implementations;
* gradual erosion of knowledge quality in the trusted layer.

That is not an acceptable long-term architecture.

## Decision

DevLog adopts a **trusted knowledge duplicate policy** that clearly separates:

* acceptable repetition in proposal history;
* unacceptable redundancy in trusted knowledge;
* primary upstream prevention responsibilities;
* downstream safety-net responsibilities;
* remediation of already-existing duplicate debt.

### 1. Proposal History And Trusted Knowledge Are Different Policy Domains

Per ADR-006, proposal history is an immutable record of analysis and validation
lifecycle.

Two proposals may express highly similar or even equivalent ideas while still
representing distinct lifecycle events.

That can be acceptable.

Trusted knowledge has a different role.

It is the durable project memory used for:

* human understanding;
* future knowledge selection;
* future AI / agent context;
* project-state interpretation.

Equivalent trusted knowledge should therefore not accumulate casually.

The system must not treat duplicate proposal history and duplicate trusted
knowledge as the same class of problem.

### 2. Duplicate Categories Must Be Distinguished Explicitly

The following categories are recognized at architectural level.

#### Exact Duplicate Proposal

Two proposals carry materially identical knowledge content, but appear as
distinct proposal records.

This may be acceptable when they correspond to different analysis runs or
lifecycle events.

#### Repeated Proposal With Legitimate Distinct History

Two similar proposals exist because:

* the repository was reanalyzed;
* validation outcomes differed;
* the proposal was reconsidered in a new context;
* or the system intentionally preserved history.

This is acceptable under ADR-006 unless a narrower implementation decision says
otherwise for a specific proposal family.

#### Exact Trusted-Knowledge Duplicate

Two trusted knowledge records in the same project express the same semantic
statement with no meaningful lifecycle, temporal, or provenance distinction.

This is not acceptable as steady-state trusted knowledge.

#### Semantic Near-Duplicate Trusted Knowledge

Two trusted knowledge records are not textually identical but convey
substantially the same meaning.

This is generally undesirable and should be prevented where confidence is high,
while preserving human review for ambiguous cases.

#### Legitimate Enrichment

A new trusted statement meaningfully extends, refines, or contextualizes an
existing trusted statement without merely restating it.

This is acceptable and should not be misclassified as duplication.

#### Historically Distinct Successor

A newer statement may represent a changed current state while an older statement
remains historically true.

This is not a duplicate merely because both touch the same domain topic.

Historical truth must remain preservable per ADR-050.

### 3. Trusted Knowledge Should Minimize Redundant Semantic Restatement

DevLog must optimize for:

more useful evidence → better understanding

not:

more analyses → more semantically repetitive trusted records

The trusted layer should therefore prefer:

* `NO_SIGNIFICANT_DELTA` or no proposal when nothing materially new is learned;
* `ENRICHES` when prior trusted knowledge remains valid but incomplete;
* explicit future contradiction / supersession lifecycle when current knowledge
  truly changes;
* a new trusted record only when genuinely new knowledge exists.

### 4. Human Review Must Not Be The Primary Duplicate-Control Mechanism

The human reviewer remains important, but not as the main line of defense.

Relying primarily on the human to notice duplicates is too weak because it:

* makes quality depend on vigilance;
* creates inconsistent outcomes;
* scales poorly as trusted knowledge grows;
* undermines incremental knowledge evolution.

Human review should instead:

* resolve ambiguous near-duplicate cases;
* approve legitimate enrichments;
* arbitrate remediation actions when history or meaning is unclear.

### 5. Upstream Prevention Is The Primary Line Of Defense

The preferred architectural boundary is to reduce duplicate proposals before
they reach trusted persistence.

Upstream prevention may use:

* bounded trusted knowledge context;
* delta-oriented prompt / contract semantics;
* intent-scoped selection;
* no-proposal outcomes when no meaningful delta exists.

This reduces:

* redundant proposal churn;
* validator noise;
* accidental trusted duplication.

### 6. Downstream Safeguards Are Still Required

Upstream prevention is necessary but not sufficient.

The Core must still protect trusted persistence with downstream safeguards before
or during acceptance / promotion.

The initial enforcement posture is:

* hard-block obvious exact trusted duplicates;
* warn or soft-block strong semantic near-duplicates where confidence is high;
* escalate ambiguous overlap to human judgment;
* never silently auto-merge trusted knowledge without an approved lifecycle.

This keeps the trusted layer protected even when analysis-time controls miss.

### 7. Minimum Comparison Signals Must Be Structured And Deterministic

Future implementation should at minimum consider:

* project scope;
* proposal family / trusted knowledge family;
* normalized knowledge type;
* `sourceType`;
* enrichment target identity when present;
* title similarity;
* summary / content similarity;
* rationale overlap;
* evidence overlap;
* accepted relation context where relevant.

This ADR does not prescribe a final matching algorithm.

It defines the minimum semantic comparison dimensions that later Stories may use
to implement policy safely.

### 8. Existing Duplicates Are Development Debt, Not Target Behavior

Trusted-knowledge duplicates already present in the database may be tolerated
temporarily during development.

However, their presence must not be interpreted as acceptable target-state
behavior.

Existing duplicates are remediation debt.

They should be:

* identifiable;
* classifiable;
* reviewable;
* remediated through explicit workflow rather than silent destructive cleanup.

### 9. Prevention, Enforcement, And Remediation Remain Separate Concerns

The lifecycle is intentionally split:

* policy definition;
* prevention and enforcement for new duplicate creation;
* audit and remediation of existing duplicate stock.

This separation keeps responsibilities clear:

* policy clarifies the target semantics;
* prevention protects future writes;
* remediation cleans historical debt.

### 10. Duplicate Policy Must Preserve Historical Truth And Lifecycle Traceability

A newer knowledge statement must not erase historically meaningful prior
knowledge merely because the two statements appear related.

Likewise, deduplication must not collapse lifecycle history in a way that hides:

* prior accepted proposals;
* past evidence;
* earlier repository states;
* human decisions.

Trusted-knowledge hygiene must improve signal quality without rewriting the
project’s engineering history.

## Consequences

### Positive

* DevLog gains an explicit architectural answer to the duplicate-policy
  question.
* Future Stories can implement duplicate prevention without inventing their own
  semantics.
* Trusted knowledge quality becomes a system responsibility rather than a
  reviewer habit.
* Incremental knowledge evolution gains a clearer operational target.

### Negative / Trade-Offs

* Semantic near-duplicate handling remains heuristic and may require careful
  tuning.
* The policy increases implementation complexity in Story 0039.
* Existing duplicate debt still requires a separate remediation workflow.

### Follow-Up

* Story 0039 implements prevention and downstream safeguards for new duplicate
  creation.
* Story 0040 audits and remediates the existing trusted duplicate stock.

## Non-Goals

This ADR does not require:

* embeddings;
* vector databases;
* a graph database;
* automatic trusted-knowledge mutation by AI;
* silent auto-acceptance of duplicate-resolution actions;
* a full contradiction lifecycle;
* a full supersession lifecycle;
* a temporal knowledge engine;
* a frontend redesign.
